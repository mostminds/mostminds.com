Castles

Castles have three levels: stairs (1) lead up to the wall-walks (2), which connect to the towers (3). Melee can't reach more than one level up, and shooters gain range with height. Gates open only for their owner; anything with hit points can be broken down.

Buildings and cover

Guns and siege weapons

A gun fires for the side with troops standing next to it and no enemies there. Kill the crew and step up to capture it. Ships and mortars can knock guns out.

Place units by hand

Pick a tool, then tap the ground. Erase removes objects and hand-placed units. Units added in the army list deploy automatically.

Sea map

Forts

Forts fire in every direction. A ship whose crew outnumbers the garrison can come alongside and storm the fort; a fort whose garrison falls changes sides.

Your 3D models

Load models you've exported from your own tools and assign them to a unit, ship, creature or building. Select the model file together with any files it uses: .glb; .gltf with its .bin and textures; .obj with its .mtl and textures; .ply (vertex colours); or .fbx. Models are scaled to fit the unit; clips named walk, attack, death and idle play automatically. They stay in this browser only.

    Model library

    Add a whole folder of models you've exported, then search it by name. Assign puts a model on the target chosen above. Auto-match replaces every unit, ship, creature and building whose name matches a file name. To control the pairing, include a mapping.json in the folder, such as {"my_knight.glb": "knight", "big_wall.obj": "struct:castleWall"}.

      Import your own units

      Paste JSON stat blocks. Plain objects become land units; use {"units":{}, "ships":{}, "creatures":{}} to add naval entries too. Fields match the built-in roster: hp att str def rng mag style speed range move size bonus abilities.
